# p03

**Prompt the founder typed:**
> the settings page feels cluttered and I hate looking at it. there are like 14 rows now and half of them are things nobody touches. clean it up

_Context: Just came back to a settings screen that has grown organically for months_

For EACH set below, judge EACH pattern: is it genuinely relevant to this specific prompt — would surfacing it change or improve how an agent handles this request? Be strict. A pattern that is merely topically adjacent is NOT relevant.


## Set A

- **Unlabeled Working Surface** — Surfaces "no user sees" — shell prompts, admin panels, log output, internal dashboards — are used by humans under time pressure, and identical-looking contexts there produce identity confusion at the exact moment stakes are highest. Unseen does not mean unused; it means untested. TRIGGER: Work touches an internal-only surface — CLI output, shell/SSH tooling, admin console, staging vs prod environment, log lines, migration scripts — and the change is justified as "internal, no design needed."
- **Craft On The Wrong Axis** — Engineering and design effort has a fixed budget and no automatic mapping to user-perceived value; teams that measure craft by internal difficulty will pour it into components whose quality is invisible in use, and ship a product whose expensive parts are the ones nobody experiences. TRIGGER: A diff or spec invests heavy effort in something the user never sees, touches, or measures — an internal abstraction, a component's hidden tolerances, a custom build pipeline, a rendering engine — while the user-facing behavior is unchanged.
- **Product Line Sprawl** — Each additional surface, SKU, or entry point consumes maintenance, documentation, support, and decision bandwidth permanently, while the marginal one is almost always worse than the median — so average quality falls monotonically as count rises, and the team's best people are spread across things no one would start today. TRIGGER: The plan adds a new surface, product, SKU, CLI command, MCP tool, page, or entry point while existing ones are unfinished, unowned, or undocumented; a countable surface inventory grows.
- **The Great Rewrite** — A rewrite discards the accumulated bug fixes encoded in the old code — each ugly branch is a defect someone found in production — while the old system keeps needing maintenance, so the team pays for two systems and ships neither. TRIGGER: A prompt or issue proposing to "rewrite from scratch", "start clean", "greenfield it", or replace a working subsystem wholesale because the code is messy.
- **Preserving The Shape** — When a change is bolted onto an existing structure specifically to avoid disturbing it — because touching it would trigger a migration, recertification, or retraining cost — the new behavior inherits constraints it was never designed for, and the reason to avoid them becomes a reason not to fix them. TRIGGER: A change explicitly scoped to avoid touching an existing schema, interface, migration, or retraining/re-certification cost — phrases like "without changing the existing", "to avoid a migration", "keep it backward compatible" driving the design rather than following it.
- **Distributed Monolith** — Splitting a codebase into services that share a release cadence and shared libraries converts in-process function calls into network calls without decoupling deploys, so you pay partial-failure, latency, and operational cost while keeping every coupling you had. TRIGGER: A spec proposing a separate service, worker, repo, or deployable per entity/destination/integration, or a diff introducing an RPC boundary between two things that ship together.
- **Motion Without An Exit** — Vestibular responses to large-field motion are physiological, not preferential — a transition that reads as delightful to most users induces genuine nausea and vertigo in a minority, and without a system-level opt-out those users cannot use the product at all. TRIGGER: A diff adds parallax, zoom transitions, autoplay, scroll-linked animation, or page transitions — and does not gate them behind prefers-reduced-motion or an equivalent user setting.
- **Feature Parity Positioning** — Positioning your product as a better version of an incumbent asks the customer to compare on the incumbent's chosen axes, where the incumbent's scale wins by construction, and it gives no one a reason to bear switching cost. Parity is also unbounded work: matching the feature list is a treadmill the incumbent sets the speed of. TRIGGER: A positioning doc, landing page, or spec that frames the product as "X but better/faster/cheaper", or leads with a feature-comparison table against a named incumbent.
- **The Metric That Moves Alone** — A proxy metric that can rise without the underlying job being served will be optimized directly, because it is the thing that is measured and reported; the product then drifts toward whatever produces the number, and the gap between metric and job widens fastest exactly when the metric looks best. TRIGGER: A success criterion is a count that can increase while the underlying job goes unserved — events emitted, broadcasts sent, tool calls, DAU, sign-ups, page views — with no paired measure of whether the recipient acted on it.
- **Seat Pricing Punishes Adoption** — When a product's value rises with how many colleagues are on it but the price also rises per colleague, the buyer's rational move is to restrict the rollout — which starves the exact network effect the product depends on. The pricing model actively fights the product's mechanism, and the resulting low penetration reads to the team as weak demand. TRIGGER: A pricing page or spec charging per seat, per user, or per member for a product whose core value requires a whole team, or charging full price for read-only/viewer/observer roles.
- **The Famous Forecaster** — Confidence and reputation are produced by a different process than accuracy, and in public settings they are rewarded more, so the loudest forecast is systematically the least hedged. Without a resolution date and a scoring rule, no forecast ever gets marked wrong, and the calibration signal never reaches anyone. TRIGGER: A claim about the future (adoption, competitor behavior, market timing, "users will want X") is backed by seniority, conviction, or a cited authority rather than by a dated, falsifiable statement someone will check.
- **Alert As The Fix** — Adding a warning to an unactioned stream does not create action; it raises the base rate of ignorable signals, which lowers the probability that any specific one is investigated. The detection exists on paper and is absent in practice. TRIGGER: A proposed remediation that consists of logging a warning, emitting a metric, or adding a monitor — with no owner, threshold, or defined response — or a codebase with existing warnings nobody triages.
- **Redesign Riding A Rewrite** — Shipping a new surface on top of a new backend at once destroys attribution and rollback simultaneously: you cannot tell whether users hate the design or the bugs, and you cannot revert the design because the old one no longer has a system underneath it. TRIGGER: A plan couples a visual/IA redesign with a platform migration, framework swap, schema change, or API rewrite in one release — or the rollback plan for a UI change is "revert the deploy" when the data model also changed.
- **The Inside-View Estimate** — An estimate built by decomposing the specific task counts only the steps you can currently see, and the steps you cannot see are exactly the ones that consume the schedule. Decomposition feels more rigorous than a base rate, so it displaces the base rate rather than being checked against it. TRIGGER: A plan, spec, or roadmap states a duration, ship date, or phase count derived by listing the work, with no reference to how long a comparable past effort actually took.
- **Distribution As An Afterthought** — Product work has a legible feedback loop (ship, see it work) and distribution does not, so engineering-led teams spend the entire runway on the loop that feels productive and reach the market with no channel built. By the time distribution becomes urgent, the money that would have funded channel experiments is already spent on the product. TRIGGER: A roadmap, spec, or milestone list where every item is a product capability and no item names a specific acquisition channel, or a plan that says marketing/launch/GTM happens "after v1".

## Set B

- **Signifier Erasure** — Flattening removes borders, fills, and shadows — which are not decoration but the clickability cues users scan for — so the visual noise drops while the search cost rises; the page looks calmer in a screenshot and costs more per task in use. TRIGGER: A diff removes borders, background fills, underlines, or shadows from interactive elements, converts buttons to text/ghost buttons, or a spec asks to "make it cleaner / flatter / less chrome."
- **Relearning Tax Ignored** — In a product people use daily, the interface is a motor habit; moving things resets that habit for every existing user at once, so the cost is paid on day one by everyone while the benefit accrues slowly to newcomers who do not exist yet. The new-user gain almost never exceeds the aggregate relearning loss. TRIGGER: A spec reorganizes navigation, moves an existing feature to a new location, renames a primary action, or changes the information architecture of a product that already has habituated daily users.
- **Numbers Nobody Measured** — A quantitative claim with no measurement behind it still functions as one downstream — it gets cited, budgeted against, and used to close arguments — because the number's format carries authority its provenance does not. Organizations generate these numbers where the desired answer is known and the measurement is expensive. TRIGGER: A spec, README, or marketing line quotes a reliability, latency, uptime, accuracy, or coverage figure without naming the measurement that produced it.
- **Refusing The Amputation** — Deferring a feature to 'phase 2' keeps it in the architecture — its data model, its surface area, its coupling — so the v1 carries the full complexity cost while delivering none of the value. Deletion is the only scope cut that actually reduces work; deferral is bookkeeping. TRIGGER: A v1 spec preserves every feature from a prototype or predecessor, and the scope-cut discussion produces deferrals ('phase 2', 'later slice', 'roadmap') rather than removals from the model.
- **Chesterton's Dead Code** — Code that appears unused is often reachable through a path nobody has in mind — an old flag, a stale server, a legacy client — so removing the guard while leaving the code, or reusing its switch for a new meaning, arms a landmine that only fires under partial deployment. TRIGGER: A diff that deletes or repurposes a feature flag, retry, guard clause, or branch labeled unused/deprecated/legacy without explaining what originally set it.
- **Control Swallowed By Screen** — A screen control demands visual attention and offers no tactile confirmation, so moving a frequent or eyes-busy action into a menu converts a sub-second muscle-memory operation into a look-navigate-aim-verify loop. The cost is invisible in a demo where the user is already looking at the screen. TRIGGER: A spec relocates a frequent, urgent, or eyes-busy control into a screen, menu, submenu, command palette, or gesture — replacing a dedicated key, button, or always-visible affordance.
- **Discoverability Mistaken For Absence** — Users request features they cannot find, so a request queue reads as a gap list when it is often a findability report; building the requested thing a second time adds surface area without adding capability and makes the original even harder to find. TRIGGER: A ticket or spec proposes building a capability that may already exist somewhere in the product — "add a way to X," "users can't do Y" — without a check of whether X/Y already ships behind a menu, flag, or command.
- **The Big-Bang Relaunch** — Replacing a shipped surface wholesale in one release couples a hundred independent bets into a single irreversible event, so you cannot tell which change caused the loss, and existing users pay the full re-learning cost on a day they did not choose. Habitual users are the ones who notice most, and they are the ones you can least afford to lose. TRIGGER: A plan replaces an existing shipped surface in one release — 'v2 replaces v1', 'retire the old frame', 'cut over' — with no dual-run, opt-in, or staged migration.
- **Craft On The Wrong Axis** — Engineering and design effort has a fixed budget and no automatic mapping to user-perceived value; teams that measure craft by internal difficulty will pour it into components whose quality is invisible in use, and ship a product whose expensive parts are the ones nobody experiences. TRIGGER: A diff or spec invests heavy effort in something the user never sees, touches, or measures — an internal abstraction, a component's hidden tolerances, a custom build pipeline, a rendering engine — while the user-facing behavior is unchanged.
- **Two-Way Door Deliberated** — Applying heavyweight process to a reversible decision costs more than being wrong would have, and the delay itself removes the cheapest source of information — shipping it and watching. Organizations default to the heavy process for everything because the process was designed around the rare irreversible case. TRIGGER: An easily reversible change — copy, layout, a default value, ordering, a flag's initial setting, a threshold — is escalated to a spec, a review cycle, or a founder decision.
- **Stated Preference Trap** — Users accurately report what they want and cannot report what it costs, because the cost (latency, density, decision load) is a property of the system, not of the wish. Delivering the requested thing literally can move the metric the wrong way. TRIGGER: A spec justifies a feature with "users asked for it," a feature-request count, survey results, or a support-ticket theme — with no statement of what the addition costs in load time, screen density, or steps.
- **Curated Demo Data** — Designs validated on hand-picked content encode assumptions about length, density, freshness, and correctness that real data violates on day one; the demo proves the layout, not the product, and the gap surfaces publicly at launch scale. TRIGGER: A design, screenshot, or demo uses seeded, curated, or short sample data; or a spec has no defined empty state, long-string, stale-data, error, and high-volume case for the surface being built.
- **Accessibility As Post-Ship Bug** — Accessibility is decided by structure — semantics, focus order, names, contrast — which is fixed at the moment the component is built; deferring it converts a design constraint into a rewrite, and in many jurisdictions into legal exposure that no backlog priority can defer. TRIGGER: A spec or diff ships an interactive component with a11y noted as follow-up, or introduces custom interactive markup (div-as-button, custom modal, canvas UI, drag-and-drop) with no keyboard path, focus management, or accessible name.
- **Metric As Taste Proxy** — Delegating small aesthetic judgments to experiments converts every decision into a measurement cost, and because micro-variants have micro-effects, the org optimizes a local hill it can measure while losing the ability to make any decision it cannot. Designers who supply judgment become unable to justify their work and leave. TRIGGER: A design disagreement is proposed to be settled by A/B test, click-through data, or "let's measure it" — particularly on typography, spacing, color, copy tone, or single-pixel values.
- **Native Control Replacement** — Platform controls carry years of accumulated behavior — keyboard, IME, screen readers, touch targets, zoom, autofill — that is invisible in the visual spec; a custom reimplementation reproduces the appearance and silently drops the behavior, and the loss shows up only for users you are not. TRIGGER: A diff replaces a native element (select, date input, file input, scrollbar, checkbox, alert) with a custom component, or a design spec styles a control in a way only achievable by rebuilding it.

## Set C

- **The Never-Restarted Subsystem** — Long-lived services grow past the scale their cold-start path was designed for, and because that path never runs, the regression is invisible until the day you need it. Recovery time then becomes an unknown discovered during the outage. TRIGGER: A design involving a long-lived stateful service, cache, or index, where warm-up, rebuild, replay, or cold-start time is unstated or assumed fast.
- **The Metric That Moves Alone** — A proxy metric that can rise without the underlying job being served will be optimized directly, because it is the thing that is measured and reported; the product then drifts toward whatever produces the number, and the gap between metric and job widens fastest exactly when the metric looks best. TRIGGER: A success criterion is a count that can increase while the underlying job goes unserved — events emitted, broadcasts sent, tool calls, DAU, sign-ups, page views — with no paired measure of whether the recipient acted on it.
- **The Approval Bottleneck** — Centralizing a recurring decision in one person converts a throughput problem into a queue whose depth is invisible to the approver, who sees only the items they touched. Quality rises per item while the volume of items that never arrive — because the wait killed them — goes unmeasured. TRIGGER: A process or spec routes every instance of a recurring decision (hires, merges, deploys, copy changes, place bindings) through one named approver, with no delegation rule or bypass condition.
- **Paid Launch Without A Loop** — Advertising rents attention; it does not build any mechanism whereby one user produces the next. Without an in-product loop (sharing, referral, invite, content, SEO surface), the acquisition curve is a linear function of spend with no residual, so growth stops the instant the budget does — and the ad spend never revealed whether anyone would have come organically. TRIGGER: A GTM plan with a large paid media budget, a launch-day ad buy, or a campaign brief, where no line item describes an in-product mechanism that generates the next user.
- **Second-System Rewrite** — A rewrite is scoped against the accumulated wishlist of everything the old system got wrong, so the new system is strictly larger than the one that already took years. Meanwhile the old system keeps shipping nothing, and the rewrite's real cost is the undocumented edge cases embedded in the code being discarded. TRIGGER: A proposal to rewrite a working module or surface from scratch, justified by cruft, where the new version is also scoped to fix known limitations of the old one.
- **Refusing The Amputation** — Deferring a feature to 'phase 2' keeps it in the architecture — its data model, its surface area, its coupling — so the v1 carries the full complexity cost while delivering none of the value. Deletion is the only scope cut that actually reduces work; deferral is bookkeeping. TRIGGER: A v1 spec preserves every feature from a prototype or predecessor, and the scope-cut discussion produces deferrals ('phase 2', 'later slice', 'roadmap') rather than removals from the model.
- **Prompt Inflation** — Each interruption's cost is paid by every user on every occurrence, while its benefit accrues only in the rare case it prevents; past a low frequency threshold users learn to dismiss the class reflexively, which destroys the signal value of the one prompt that mattered. TRIGGER: A diff adds a confirmation dialog, permission prompt, toast, banner, badge, or notification to a flow that already has one — or a spec's answer to a risk is "warn the user."
- **Confirmation Instead Of Reversal** — An "Are you sure?" dialog is a speed bump, not a safeguard — users confirm the action they believe they are taking, so the dialog passes exactly when the user is already wrong. Only a reversal path bounds the damage after the mistaken confirm. TRIGGER: A diff or spec guards a destructive, irreversible, or broadcast-to-others action with a confirmation dialog, checkbox, or type-to-confirm — and no undo window, recall, dry-run, or reversal path is specified.
- **Stated Preference Trap** — Users accurately report what they want and cannot report what it costs, because the cost (latency, density, decision load) is a property of the system, not of the wish. Delivering the requested thing literally can move the metric the wrong way. TRIGGER: A spec justifies a feature with "users asked for it," a feature-request count, survey results, or a support-ticket theme — with no statement of what the addition costs in load time, screen density, or steps.
- **Compliance As Friction** — A founder who correctly identifies a requirement as wasteful can build a tool that removes the appearance of compliance rather than the requirement, because the tool is faster to build than the argument to win. The evidence of the workaround is durable, discoverable, and personally attributable. TRIGGER: A prompt or diff proposes bypassing, auto-satisfying, or faking a legal, licensing, security-review, audit-log, or approval requirement on the grounds that the requirement is overhead.
- **Preference-First Framing** — When the decision-maker states their conclusion before soliciting input, every subsequent contribution is scored by the contributor against the cost of contradicting them, not against the evidence. Input arrives, so the process looks consultative, but it has been filtered to what survives disagreement with the boss. TRIGGER: A request to review, sign off on, or implement an approach whose author has already stated a preferred outcome, or a message shaped as "here's what we're doing — thoughts?"
- **Org Chart Shipped To Users** — A split that mirrors an internal boundary — separate app, brand, login, repo, or team — makes users pay the cost of a structure that exists for the builders' convenience. Users experience one job; splitting it forces them to re-onboard into a second product to accomplish what was previously one motion. TRIGGER: A split, rename, or separation in the product mirrors an internal boundary (two teams, two repos, two services, two panes) and forces the user to move between surfaces to complete one task.
- **The Deck Instead Of The Memo** — Bullets let a proposal omit the connective tissue — the constraint, the alternative rejected, the thing that would make this wrong — because a bullet has no grammar that requires them. The gaps are invisible to the reader and often to the author, who mistakes the deck's completeness for the argument's. TRIGGER: A plan or spec arrives as a feature list, bullet outline, table, or phase grid with no prose section stating the problem, the constraints, and what evidence would falsify the approach.
- **Interface Without A Contract** — When two components exchange bare numbers or strings, the meaning (unit, timezone, encoding, ID namespace) lives only in each side's head. Nothing at the boundary can detect disagreement, so the mismatch propagates as plausible-looking data instead of an error. TRIGGER: A new API field, event payload, or cross-team/cross-service boundary passing raw numbers, timestamps, identifiers, or strings with no shared type, unit, or validation at the boundary.
- **Preserving The Shape** — When a change is bolted onto an existing structure specifically to avoid disturbing it — because touching it would trigger a migration, recertification, or retraining cost — the new behavior inherits constraints it was never designed for, and the reason to avoid them becomes a reason not to fix them. TRIGGER: A change explicitly scoped to avoid touching an existing schema, interface, migration, or retraining/re-certification cost — phrases like "without changing the existing", "to avoid a migration", "keep it backward compatible" driving the design rather than following it.
