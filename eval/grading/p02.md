# p02

**Prompt the founder typed:**
> Should we build the team/multi-seat plan before launch or ship single-user first? Right now billing assumes one person per account and adding seats means touching auth, the invite flow, and the Stripe integration. I keep going back and forth. We have no paying customers yet.

_Context: Two weeks from a small launch, deciding what makes the cut_

For EACH set below, judge EACH pattern: is it genuinely relevant to this specific prompt — would surfacing it change or improve how an agent handles this request? Be strict. A pattern that is merely topically adjacent is NOT relevant.


## Set A

- **Statistics Against Trust** — Answering a reported defect with its probability treats the user's problem as frequency when the actual damage is to the belief that the product's outputs can be relied on without checking. The probability argument is usually correct and always escalates, because it tells the reporter their experience does not count. TRIGGER: A response to a user-reported bug, data-loss report, or wrong-output complaint leads with how rare the condition is, or proposes closing it as won't-fix on frequency grounds.
- **Sunk Identity** — A decision defended by prior investment or by "this is what we are" cannot be evaluated, because both premises are facts about the past rather than about the option. The incumbent option gets an unearned advantage precisely in proportion to how much was spent establishing it. TRIGGER: A decision or spec is defended by how much has already been built, how long it took, or by identity language ("we're a X company", "this is our thesis"), rather than by the option's forward value.
- **Automatic Failover Split-Brain** — Automated leader promotion cannot distinguish a dead primary from an unreachable one, so a brief network partition produces two writable primaries. The outage is then not the downtime but the reconciliation of divergent writes, which is manual and unbounded. TRIGGER: A design adding automatic failover, leader election, multi-region writes, or a health-check-driven promotion — or any mention of a heartbeat timeout that decides who is authoritative.
- **The Inside-View Estimate** — An estimate built by decomposing the specific task counts only the steps you can currently see, and the steps you cannot see are exactly the ones that consume the schedule. Decomposition feels more rigorous than a base rate, so it displaces the base rate rather than being checked against it. TRIGGER: A plan, spec, or roadmap states a duration, ship date, or phase count derived by listing the work, with no reference to how long a comparable past effort actually took.
- **Chasing The Moving Baseline** — Rescoping in-flight work to adopt a newer substrate resets the completion clock while the substrate keeps advancing, so the gap between what you have and what is current never closes — each adoption is individually justified and the sequence never terminates. TRIGGER: In-flight work is rescoped to adopt a newly released model, framework, engine, API version, or internal abstraction before the current version has shipped to anyone.
- **Premature Automation** — Automating a process before doing it manually encodes a guess about the rules into code, and the code then prevents you from observing the exceptions that would have taught you the real rules. Manual work at small N is not a stopgap, it is the only instrument that reads the requirement. TRIGGER: A spec automates onboarding, setup, matching, curation, routing, or support before that process has been performed by hand enough times to have written down the exception cases.
- **The Deck Instead Of The Memo** — Bullets let a proposal omit the connective tissue — the constraint, the alternative rejected, the thing that would make this wrong — because a bullet has no grammar that requires them. The gaps are invisible to the reader and often to the author, who mistakes the deck's completeness for the argument's. TRIGGER: A plan or spec arrives as a feature list, bullet outline, table, or phase grid with no prose section stating the problem, the constraints, and what evidence would falsify the approach.
- **The Metric Becomes The Job** — Tying reward to a single countable output makes the cheapest path to the number, not the outcome, the rational behavior for everyone measured — and the cheapest path is usually fabrication or reclassification. The metric keeps improving while the thing it proxied for degrades, and the metric is the only instrument you have. TRIGGER: A spec, OKR, or plan ties compensation, ranking, or a pass/fail gate to a single countable output (accounts, PRs merged, tickets closed, coverage percent, broadcasts sent).
- **Accessibility As Post-Ship Bug** — Accessibility is decided by structure — semantics, focus order, names, contrast — which is fixed at the moment the component is built; deferring it converts a design constraint into a rewrite, and in many jurisdictions into legal exposure that no backlog priority can defer. TRIGGER: A spec or diff ships an interactive component with a11y noted as follow-up, or introduces custom interactive markup (div-as-button, custom modal, canvas UI, drag-and-drop) with no keyboard path, focus management, or accessible name.
- **Launch Without Load** — Capacity assumptions that are never measured are wrong in the unsafe direction, because designers estimate from the intended usage rather than the arrival pattern. Load appears as a step function at launch, not a ramp, so the first real test is also the first outage. TRIGGER: A ship/launch date with no load or soak test in the plan, or a stated concurrency/throughput number that is an estimate rather than a measurement.
- **The Never-Restarted Subsystem** — Long-lived services grow past the scale their cold-start path was designed for, and because that path never runs, the regression is invisible until the day you need it. Recovery time then becomes an unknown discovered during the outage. TRIGGER: A design involving a long-lived stateful service, cache, or index, where warm-up, rebuild, replay, or cold-start time is unstated or assumed fast.
- **Preference-First Framing** — When the decision-maker states their conclusion before soliciting input, every subsequent contribution is scored by the contributor against the cost of contradicting them, not against the evidence. Input arrives, so the process looks consultative, but it has been filtered to what survives disagreement with the boss. TRIGGER: A request to review, sign off on, or implement an approach whose author has already stated a preferred outcome, or a message shaped as "here's what we're doing — thoughts?"
- **Error Text From Inside** — A message written from the system's state names a condition the user has no model for and no action attached to; it is technically accurate and operationally useless, so the user's only remaining move is to ask a human or abandon the task. TRIGGER: A diff adds or changes user-visible error, empty, or status copy that names an internal condition (state names, error codes, subsystem names, exception text) instead of what failed plus one concrete action the user can take.
- **Stated Preference Trap** — Users accurately report what they want and cannot report what it costs, because the cost (latency, density, decision load) is a property of the system, not of the wish. Delivering the requested thing literally can move the metric the wrong way. TRIGGER: A spec justifies a feature with "users asked for it," a feature-request count, survey results, or a support-ticket theme — with no statement of what the addition costs in load time, screen density, or steps.
- **Product Without Distribution** — Product quality and reach are independent variables, and quality has no mechanism to produce reach on its own — a plan composed entirely of product work therefore converges on an excellent thing nobody can find, while the burn clock runs at the same rate either way. TRIGGER: A plan, sprint, or milestone is entirely product surface — polish, refactor, design review, quality bar — with no line item for how anyone discovers, installs, or is onboarded to it, or the distribution path is known to 404.

## Set B

- **Free Tier With No Wall** — A free plan converts only where its limit sits exactly at the point a serious user becomes dependent; limits on axes casual users never hit (storage, history, time) give away the whole product and leave the paid tier selling nothing. Fixing this later means taking something back from existing users, which is a far more expensive act than setting the wall correctly at launch. TRIGGER: A spec adding a free plan, free tier, or open beta whose limits are storage, seats-of-one, retention window, or time, rather than the specific capability a committed user cannot work without; also any "free forever" framing.
- **Seat Pricing Punishes Adoption** — When a product's value rises with how many colleagues are on it but the price also rises per colleague, the buyer's rational move is to restrict the rollout — which starves the exact network effect the product depends on. The pricing model actively fights the product's mechanism, and the resulting low penetration reads to the team as weak demand. TRIGGER: A pricing page or spec charging per seat, per user, or per member for a product whose core value requires a whole team, or charging full price for read-only/viewer/observer roles.
- **Big-Bang Delivery** — When integration is scheduled once at the end, every incorrect assumption compounds silently for the whole project and all of them surface simultaneously, at the moment there is no budget left to act on them. Nothing is falsified until it is too late to be cheap. TRIGGER: A roadmap or plan whose milestones are all internal (design, build, integrate) with a single user-visible cutover at the end, and no shippable increment before it.
- **Retroactive Value-Metric Change** — Customers price their own business models on your billing unit; changing that unit — especially applying it to work already shipped — invalidates their planning and reads as a breach rather than a price rise. The revolt costs more in trust and churn than the new revenue was ever worth, because the affected customers are by definition your most successful ones. TRIGGER: A pricing change that alters the billing unit (seats→usage, revenue→installs, flat→metered), reprices existing contracts, or applies to activity that already happened.
- **Skipping The Unscalable** — Automating the first-user experience before doing it by hand encodes your guesses about what users need into software, and the automation then hides the disconfirming evidence. Manual onboarding is not inefficiency; it is the only instrument fine-grained enough to find the problem that the metrics summarize away. TRIGGER: A plan to build self-serve signup, an onboarding wizard, an email drip, an in-app tour, or a help center before roughly ten users have been onboarded by a founder in person.
- **No Beachhead** — A message aimed at everyone is specific to no one, so it fails the only test that matters early: can one user explain it to the next user in a sentence. Without a defined first customer, price, channel, and objection-handling all become guesses, and there is no dense population in which word of mouth can propagate. TRIGGER: TAM-driven sizing, "for any team / any company / everyone who...", or a launch plan listing three or more personas with no ranking of which one ships first.
- **Wartime Frame In Peacetime** — Consensus-building, broad exploration, and process are correct when the constraint is opportunity and wrong when the constraint is survival — and vice versa. Teams keep whichever mode they last succeeded in, so the mismatch persists exactly through the period where it is most expensive. TRIGGER: An existential condition is stated (runway, a competitor shipping the core feature, a broken primary flow, zero users) and the proposed response is a broad multi-track plan, a process change, or a round of alignment.
- **Friction Placed For Revenue** — Placing confirm/cancel affordances or defaults to favor the business converts an interface into a trap: the same visual language that means "proceed" elsewhere now means "you were charged," and the resulting revenue is legally recoverable and reputationally permanent. TRIGGER: A diff changes default selections, button placement/prominence, cancel-vs-confirm ordering, or opt-out wording in a purchase, subscription, consent, sharing, or account-deletion flow in a way that favors the business.
- **Control Plane Inside The Blast Radius** — When the tooling that observes, deploys, authenticates, or rolls back depends on the system it manages, the first failure removes the means of recovery. Mean time to repair is then set by whatever out-of-band path was never built. TRIGGER: A design where monitoring, deploy, auth, feature flags, or the rollback path runs on, resolves through, or authenticates against the same system it controls.
- **Distributed Monolith** — Splitting a codebase into services that share a release cadence and shared libraries converts in-process function calls into network calls without decoupling deploys, so you pay partial-failure, latency, and operational cost while keeping every coupling you had. TRIGGER: A spec proposing a separate service, worker, repo, or deployable per entity/destination/integration, or a diff introducing an RPC boundary between two things that ship together.
- **Launch Without Load** — Capacity assumptions that are never measured are wrong in the unsafe direction, because designers estimate from the intended usage rather than the arrival pattern. Load appears as a step function at launch, not a ramp, so the first real test is also the first outage. TRIGGER: A ship/launch date with no load or soak test in the plan, or a stated concurrency/throughput number that is an estimate rather than a measurement.
- **Paid Launch Without A Loop** — Advertising rents attention; it does not build any mechanism whereby one user produces the next. Without an in-product loop (sharing, referral, invite, content, SEO surface), the acquisition curve is a linear function of spend with no residual, so growth stops the instant the budget does — and the ad spend never revealed whether anyone would have come organically. TRIGGER: A GTM plan with a large paid media budget, a launch-day ad buy, or a campaign brief, where no line item describes an in-product mechanism that generates the next user.
- **Hype Ahead Of Usability** — A long, expert-narrated demo can make an unexplainable product look compelling to an audience primed to be impressed, and invite scarcity converts that impression into signups. Neither tests the actual constraint — whether one ordinary user can explain the product to the next one — so the waitlist converts into a cohort that opens it once and never returns. TRIGGER: A launch plan gated by waitlist or invite codes, or a demo script/video longer than about two minutes that requires narration to make the product legible.
- **Foundation Before First User** — Infrastructure built for a feature that does not exist has no consumer to falsify its design, so its interface is derived from imagination and calcifies before contact. The dependent feature then inherits constraints from a substrate that was never tested against the real requirement. TRIGGER: A spec adds infrastructure — an index, cache, schema, consolidation pass, worker, centroid set, router — whose only named consumer is a feature not yet built or validated.
- **Premature Scaling** — Capacity built ahead of demand converts fixed costs into a deadline: the burn rate now sets how long you have to find the demand, rather than the demand setting how much you build. Because the capacity is sunk, the team also loses the option to change the shape of the business when the early signal comes back wrong. TRIGGER: A plan that adds headcount, geographies, warehouses, regions, or provisioned infrastructure capacity on a schedule rather than in response to a measured demand signal; "build it so it scales to N" before N/100 exists.

## Set C

- **Refusing The Amputation** — Deferring a feature to 'phase 2' keeps it in the architecture — its data model, its surface area, its coupling — so the v1 carries the full complexity cost while delivering none of the value. Deletion is the only scope cut that actually reduces work; deferral is bookkeeping. TRIGGER: A v1 spec preserves every feature from a prototype or predecessor, and the scope-cut discussion produces deferrals ('phase 2', 'later slice', 'roadmap') rather than removals from the model.
- **Cold Start Ignored** — Any product whose value comes from other people's content or presence is worthless to user one, so the acquisition funnel and the retention mechanism fight each other: every user you bring sees an empty room and leaves, and their departure makes the room emptier. The founders must manufacture the initial density by hand because no channel can outrun an empty product. TRIGGER: A spec for a feed, map, marketplace, roster, leaderboard, directory, team view, or any social surface, with no stated plan for what a first user or solo user sees.
- **Seat Pricing Punishes Adoption** — When a product's value rises with how many colleagues are on it but the price also rises per colleague, the buyer's rational move is to restrict the rollout — which starves the exact network effect the product depends on. - **Growth Before Retention** — Acquisition spend against a leaky product buys a number, not a business: each cohort churns at the same rate, so the spend must repeat forever, and headcount and infrastructure scale to the peak while revenue scales to the retained fraction. The growth curve also hides the retention problem from the people funding it. TRIGGER: A plan proposes launch, marketing, headcount, or scale spend while retention, repeat usage, or activation is unmeasured, known-bad, or measured on a single cohort.
- **Boiling The Ocean** — When v1 requires three or more unproven components to all work at once, the probability of shipping is the product of each part's odds, and no single part can be validated until the others exist. Learning is deferred until the whole thing is done, which is exactly when it is most expensive to be wrong. TRIGGER: A spec or plan lists three or more novel, unproven components (new runtime, new data model, new rendering surface, new protocol) that must all work before anything is usable.
- **One-Way Door, Casual** — Decisions get processed at the weight of their immediate cost, not their reversibility, so a cheap-to-decline strategic option receives less scrutiny than an expensive-to-build feature. The irreversibility only becomes visible after the window that would have let you act on it has closed. TRIGGER: A decision that forecloses a future option (a partnership declined, a public API committed, a naming/positioning lock, a data model that cannot be migrated) is being made in the same pass as routine work, with no note distinguishing it.

- **Distribution As An Afterthought** — Product work has a legible feedback loop (ship, see it work) and distribution does not, so engineering-led teams spend the entire runway on the loop that feels productive and reach the market with no channel built. By the time distribution becomes urgent, the money that would have funded channel experiments is already spent on the product. TRIGGER: A roadmap, spec, or milestone list where every item is a product capability and no item names a specific acquisition channel, or a plan that says marketing/launch/GTM happens "after v1".

- **Two-Way Door Deliberated** — Applying heavyweight process to a reversible decision costs more than being wrong would have, and the delay itself removes the cheapest source of information — shipping it and watching. Organizations default to the heavy process for everything because the process was designed around the rare irreversible case. TRIGGER: An easily reversible change — copy, layout, a default value, ordering, a flag's initial setting, a threshold — is escalated to a spec, a review cycle, or a founder decision.
