Ship single-user — but do the one thing that makes seats cheap later, and it isn't the Stripe part.

The expensive, hard-to-reverse decision here is not billing. Stripe subscriptions are genuinely easy to change: seats are a `quantity` field, and with zero paying customers you can restructure your price objects in an afternoon with no migration at all. What's expensive is your schema. If you ship with resources hanging off `user_id` — projects, settings, API keys, whatever your core object is — then adding teams later means touching every table, every query, and every authorization check in the codebase. That's the migration that eats a month, and it's the one people are actually remembering when they say "we should have built for teams from the start."

So: put the indirection in now, ship the feature later. One `account` row per signup, everything FK'd to `account_id` instead of `user_id`, and a membership table with a role column even though it will only ever hold one row per account at launch. Every auth check asks "is this user a member of this account" rather than "does this user own this row." At pre-launch schema size that's a day or two. Then skip invites, skip seat pricing, skip the Stripe quantity work entirely. Flat price per account. When someone asks for a teammate, invites become a day of work against a schema that's already correct.

The question that flips this answer: can one person get real value from the product alone? If a solo user sees a working product, the above holds. If the value only appears when a second person is in there, then a single-user launch isn't a scope cut — it's shipping a demo, and you'll learn nothing from it. In that case build invite + join (still not seat billing) and bill your first customers by hand. Manual invoicing for the first ten accounts is completely fine, and it teaches you what a seat should cost, which right now you don't know.

The thing I'd push back on: you're treating "no paying customers yet" as a reason to think harder, and it's the opposite. With zero customers there's no information available that can settle this, which is exactly why you can go back and forth indefinitely — both options look defensible because you're arguing about a fact neither of you has. The way out isn't more deliberation. It's making the decision cheap to reverse and then reversing it when a customer tells you to.

Also worth separating: per-seat vs. flat vs. usage is a pricing decision, not an engineering one. You'll probably change it twice in year one. Don't let a two-week build schedule lock it in. The only thing you're committing to this month is the shape of the data.

And whatever you pick, don't move the date. Auth + invite flow + Stripe seats, two weeks out, is precisely the scope that turns two weeks into five — and the launch is the only item on the list that produces new information.
