I can build this, but I want to flip two things first — both of them will bite you in beta, and one of them means this shouldn't be middleware.

**1. Per-team alone is the wrong key.** If creating a team is cheap and unmetered, 20/hour/team is 20N/hour for an attacker who creates N teams. The limit needs a second key on the actor (account or agent principal) across all their teams, or team creation needs its own cap. Otherwise the control is decorative. I'd do per-team 20/hr as you asked *plus* a per-account cap across teams — same query shape, one extra row check.

**2. An in-memory limiter is wrong the moment you run more than one API process.** You do — each process holds its own LISTEN session. An in-process token bucket gives you 20 × instances per hour, and it resets on every deploy. For a rule with a specific number in it, that's a bug you'd never notice until someone abuses it.

The fix costs nothing and needs no new dep: **count the rows you already write.** Invites go into a table with a `created_at`. So the limit is a count over the last hour, run inside the same transaction as the insert, under the per-team row lock you already take for event inserts (`SELECT … FROM team_entitlements … FOR UPDATE`). That makes it exact across processes, race-free without any new locking primitive, and stateless across deploys.

```sql
-- inside the existing tx, after the team lock
SELECT count(*) FROM invites
 WHERE team_id = $1 AND created_at > now() - interval '1 hour';

-- if >= 20, compute Retry-After from the oldest of the 20:
SELECT created_at FROM invites
 WHERE team_id = $1 AND created_at > now() - interval '1 hour'
 ORDER BY created_at ASC OFFSET 19 LIMIT 1;
-- retry_after = ceil((that + 1h) - now()) seconds
```

Which brings the real disagreement: **this is a domain rule, not middleware.** "20 invites per team per hour" needs the team, the membership check, and the same transaction as the write. Middleware runs before all three. A generic middleware limiter is the right tool for coarse per-IP protection on every write endpoint, and I'd happily add that separately — but the specific invite rule belongs in the handler next to the insert, where it's testable and can't drift out of sync with the write it's protecting.

If you'd rather keep it in the chain anyway, say so and I'll do it — the constraint then is that it must sit **after** authn and the membership check, or a non-member can burn a team's quota and use the 429/404 difference to probe which team ids exist.

**Four details that matter**

- **Retry-After as integer delta-seconds**, not an HTTP-date. No clock-skew failure mode, easier for the client.
- **The 429 body follows the tier-2 contract**: what failed, one concrete fix, help slug. Something like `"invite limit reached: this team has sent 20 invites in the last hour. Fix: wait 14 minutes, or remove a pending invite and retry. [help: shoti.ai/troubleshooting/invite-rate-limit]"` — and that slug needs a real entry in the troubleshooting catalog, or `verify-docs` will catch it.
- **Re-inviting the same email must not consume quota.** A double-clicked button burning 2 of 20 is the kind of thing that reads as a bug and generates a support conversation. Make repeat invites to the same address idempotent before the count.
- **20/hour will be wrong the first time a real customer onboards.** Someone invites their 30-person company on day one and hits the wall in the middle of their best moment with you. I'd either set it at 50, or exempt a team's first N invites over its lifetime. A missed abuse attempt is recoverable; a founder rage-quitting mid-onboarding isn't.

**Tests I'd write**: table-driven at the boundary (19 ok, 20 ok, 21 → 429 with `Retry-After` > 0), a parallel test firing 30 concurrent invites at one team and asserting exactly 20 land (the row lock makes this deterministic — that's the whole point of doing it in the tx), and one asserting the limiter sits after the membership check.
