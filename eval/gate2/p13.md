# p13

**Prompt:**
> read the copy on the landing page and tell me honestly if a stranger would understand what this does in ten seconds. I've read it so many times I can't tell anymore. don't be nice about it

_Context: Reviewing a landing page draft the night before sharing it_


## Candidates (6)

- **Metaphor Fusion** [warn] — A product that fuses several familiar categories inherits none of their mental models, so a new user has no prior to fall back on and no one-sentence description to pass along; adoption then depends on formal explanation, which does not travel. TRIGGER: A spec describes the surface by joining categories with "and" ("it's a doc and a chat and a board"), unifies two existing product surfaces into one, or cannot state what the thing is in one sentence a non-user would repeat.
- **Positioning To The Wrong Frame** [warn] — The category you claim determines which competitors the buyer mentally lines you up against and which features they treat as table stakes. Claim a broad, incumbent-owned category and your genuine differentiator is scored as a minor feature; claim the frame where that differentiator is the whole point and the same product becomes the obvious choice. TRIGGER: Copy or a spec that names a broad established category ("CRM", "project management", "observability", "coordination layer") without naming the specific buyer and the specific alternative they'd otherwise use.
- **Feature Parity Positioning** [warn] — Positioning your product as a better version of an incumbent asks the customer to compare on the incumbent's chosen axes, where the incumbent's scale wins by construction, and it gives no one a reason to bear switching cost. Parity is also unbounded work: matching the feature list is a treadmill the incumbent sets the speed of. TRIGGER: A positioning doc, landing page, or spec that frames the product as "X but better/faster/cheaper", or leads with a feature-comparison table against a named incumbent.
- **No Beachhead** [warn] — A message aimed at everyone is specific to no one, so it fails the only test that matters early: can one user explain it to the next user in a sentence. Without a defined first customer, price, channel, and objection-handling all become guesses, and there is no dense population in which word of mouth can propagate. TRIGGER: TAM-driven sizing, "for any team / any company / everyone who...", or a launch plan listing three or more personas with no ranking of which one ships first.
- **Numbers Nobody Measured** [block] — A quantitative claim with no measurement behind it still functions as one downstream — it gets cited, budgeted against, and used to close arguments — because the number's format carries authority its provenance does not. Organizations generate these numbers where the desired answer is known and the measurement is expensive. TRIGGER: A spec, README, or marketing line quotes a reliability, latency, uptime, accuracy, or coverage figure without naming the measurement that produced it.
- **Job Mistaken For Demographic** [note] — Segmenting by who the user is produces feature requests that correlate with the segment but explain nothing causal, so improvements aimed at the persona miss the situation that actually triggered the purchase. Two people in the same demographic hire the product for opposite jobs, and averaging them produces a product for neither. TRIGGER: Requirements are segmented by who the user is (role, persona, seniority, company size, 'founders vs engineers') rather than by the situation and task at the moment of use.
