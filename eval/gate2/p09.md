# p09

**Prompt:**
> A prospect said they'd sign up if we had a Slack integration. Just one prospect. Do I build it? It's maybe 3 days of work and I'd have to maintain it forever.

_Context: Considering adding an integration a prospect asked for_


## Candidates (5)

- **Stated Preference Trap** [warn] — Users accurately report what they want and cannot report what it costs, because the cost (latency, density, decision load) is a property of the system, not of the wish. Delivering the requested thing literally can move the metric the wrong way. TRIGGER: A spec justifies a feature with "users asked for it," a feature-request count, survey results, or a support-ticket theme — with no statement of what the addition costs in load time, screen density, or steps.
- **Product Line Sprawl** [warn] — Each additional surface, SKU, or entry point consumes maintenance, documentation, support, and decision bandwidth permanently, while the marginal one is almost always worse than the median — so average quality falls monotonically as count rises, and the team's best people are spread across things no one would start today. TRIGGER: The plan adds a new surface, product, SKU, CLI command, MCP tool, page, or entry point while existing ones are unfinished, unowned, or undocumented; a countable surface inventory grows.
- **Built On Rented Land** [warn] — A distribution channel owned by another company is revocable on their schedule, and they revoke it exactly when you become large enough to matter or when they want the market themselves. The dependency is invisible during the growth phase because the channel is working, which is the same evidence that makes you a threat. TRIGGER: A growth mechanism that depends on another company's API, social graph, app-store placement, feed distribution, marketplace listing, extension store, or OAuth integration as the primary source of new users.
- **One-Way Door, Casual** [warn] — Decisions get processed at the weight of their immediate cost, not their reversibility, so a cheap-to-decline strategic option receives less scrutiny than an expensive-to-build feature. The irreversibility only becomes visible after the window that would have let you act on it has closed. TRIGGER: A decision that forecloses a future option (a partnership declined, a public API committed, a naming/positioning lock, a data model that cannot be migrated) is being made in the same pass as routine work, with no note distinguishing it.
- **Discoverability Mistaken For Absence** [note] — Users request features they cannot find, so a request queue reads as a gap list when it is often a findability report; building the requested thing a second time adds surface area without adding capability and makes the original even harder to find. TRIGGER: A ticket or spec proposes building a capability that may already exist somewhere in the product — "add a way to X," "users can't do Y" — without a check of whether X/Y already ships behind a menu, flag, or command.
