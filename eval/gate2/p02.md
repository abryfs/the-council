# p02

**Prompt:**
> Should we build the team/multi-seat plan before launch or ship single-user first? Right now billing assumes one person per account and adding seats means touching auth, the invite flow, and the Stripe integration. I keep going back and forth. We have no paying customers yet.

_Context: Two weeks from a small launch, deciding what makes the cut_


## Candidates (8)

- **Foundation Before First User** [block] — Infrastructure built for a feature that does not exist has no consumer to falsify its design, so its interface is derived from imagination and calcifies before contact. The dependent feature then inherits constraints from a substrate that was never tested against the real requirement. TRIGGER: A spec adds infrastructure — an index, cache, schema, consolidation pass, worker, centroid set, router — whose only named consumer is a feature not yet built or validated.
- **Seat Pricing Punishes Adoption** [warn] — When a product's value rises with how many colleagues are on it but the price also rises per colleague, the buyer's rational move is to restrict the rollout — which starves the exact network effect the product depends on. The pricing model actively fights the product's mechanism, and the resulting low penetration reads to the team as weak demand. TRIGGER: A pricing page or spec charging per seat, per user, or per member for a product whose core value requires a whole team, or charging full price for read-only/viewer/observer roles.
- **Premature Scaling** [block] — Capacity built ahead of demand converts fixed costs into a deadline: the burn rate now sets how long you have to find the demand, rather than the demand setting how much you build. Because the capacity is sunk, the team also loses the option to change the shape of the business when the early signal comes back wrong. TRIGGER: A plan that adds headcount, geographies, warehouses, regions, or provisioned infrastructure capacity on a schedule rather than in response to a measured demand signal; "build it so it scales to N" before N/100 exists.
- **Cold Start Ignored** [block] — Any product whose value comes from other people's content or presence is worthless to user one, so the acquisition funnel and the retention mechanism fight each other: every user you bring sees an empty room and leaves, and their departure makes the room emptier. The founders must manufacture the initial density by hand because no channel can outrun an empty product. TRIGGER: A spec for a feed, map, marketplace, roster, leaderboard, directory, team view, or any social surface, with no stated plan for what a first user or solo user sees.
- **Refusing The Amputation** [warn] — Deferring a feature to 'phase 2' keeps it in the architecture — its data model, its surface area, its coupling — so the v1 carries the full complexity cost while delivering none of the value. Deletion is the only scope cut that actually reduces work; deferral is bookkeeping. TRIGGER: A v1 spec preserves every feature from a prototype or predecessor, and the scope-cut discussion produces deferrals ('phase 2', 'later slice', 'roadmap') rather than removals from the model.
- **Growth Before Retention** [block] — Acquisition spend against a leaky product buys a number, not a business: each cohort churns at the same rate, so the spend must repeat forever, and headcount and infrastructure scale to the peak while revenue scales to the retained fraction. The growth curve also hides the retention problem from the people funding it. TRIGGER: A plan proposes launch, marketing, headcount, or scale spend while retention, repeat usage, or activation is unmeasured, known-bad, or measured on a single cohort.
- **Retroactive Value-Metric Change** [block] — Customers price their own business models on your billing unit; changing that unit — especially applying it to work already shipped — invalidates their planning and reads as a breach rather than a price rise. The revolt costs more in trust and churn than the new revenue was ever worth, because the affected customers are by definition your most successful ones. TRIGGER: A pricing change that alters the billing unit (seats→usage, revenue→installs, flat→metered), reprices existing contracts, or applies to activity that already happened.
- **Distribution As An Afterthought** [block] — Product work has a legible feedback loop (ship, see it work) and distribution does not, so engineering-led teams spend the entire runway on the loop that feels productive and reach the market with no channel built. By the time distribution becomes urgent, the money that would have funded channel experiments is already spent on the product. TRIGGER: A roadmap, spec, or milestone list where every item is a product capability and no item names a specific acquisition channel, or a plan that says marketing/launch/GTM happens "after v1".
