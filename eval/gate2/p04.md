# p04

**Prompt:**
> we have 4 users. two of them are me and my cofounder. what now

_Context: Post-launch reality check after a Show HN that went nowhere_


## Candidates (8)

- **Skipping The Unscalable** [warn] — Automating the first-user experience before doing it by hand encodes your guesses about what users need into software, and the automation then hides the disconfirming evidence. Manual onboarding is not inefficiency; it is the only instrument fine-grained enough to find the problem that the metrics summarize away. TRIGGER: A plan to build self-serve signup, an onboarding wizard, an email drip, an in-app tour, or a help center before roughly ten users have been onboarded by a founder in person.
- **Funding As Distribution** [warn] — A funding announcement and its press cycle produce a one-time spike of curious non-customers, which the team reads as demand and uses to justify skipping channel-building. Press has no compounding mechanism — it cannot be re-run — so the traffic decays to zero while the burn rate raised to match the ambition does not. TRIGGER: A launch plan whose top-of-funnel is press coverage, a funding announcement, an influencer/analyst push, or a Product Hunt / HN / Show HN day, with no channel that keeps producing users on day 30.
- **Distribution As An Afterthought** [block] — Product work has a legible feedback loop (ship, see it work) and distribution does not, so engineering-led teams spend the entire runway on the loop that feels productive and reach the market with no channel built. By the time distribution becomes urgent, the money that would have funded channel experiments is already spent on the product. TRIGGER: A roadmap, spec, or milestone list where every item is a product capability and no item names a specific acquisition channel, or a plan that says marketing/launch/GTM happens "after v1".
- **Piggyback Channel Ignored** [note] — Early on you cannot afford to create demand, only to intercept it, which means the cheapest first channel is wherever your users are already aggregated and transacting. Teams skip this because owned channels (blog, newsletter, own social account) feel more legitimate — but those start at zero audience and compound too slowly to survive the runway. TRIGGER: A GTM plan whose channel list contains only owned surfaces (own blog, own newsletter, own social account, own docs site) and names no existing venue, marketplace, forum, or community where the target users already gather.
- **No Beachhead** [warn] — A message aimed at everyone is specific to no one, so it fails the only test that matters early: can one user explain it to the next user in a sentence. Without a defined first customer, price, channel, and objection-handling all become guesses, and there is no dense population in which word of mouth can propagate. TRIGGER: TAM-driven sizing, "for any team / any company / everyone who...", or a launch plan listing three or more personas with no ranking of which one ships first.
- **Growth Before Retention** [block] — Acquisition spend against a leaky product buys a number, not a business: each cohort churns at the same rate, so the spend must repeat forever, and headcount and infrastructure scale to the peak while revenue scales to the retained fraction. The growth curve also hides the retention problem from the people funding it. TRIGGER: A plan proposes launch, marketing, headcount, or scale spend while retention, repeat usage, or activation is unmeasured, known-bad, or measured on a single cohort.
- **Exit Reason Assumed** [note] — Churn explanations default to the most legible variable — price, salary, performance — because it is the one already instrumented. The actual reasons are relational and only obtainable by asking, so the retention fix targets the wrong lever and produces no change, which is then read as the problem being intractable. TRIGGER: An explanation of attrition, churn, drop-off, or non-adoption asserts a cause (price, speed, missing feature) without citing anything the departed users or teammates actually said.
- **Wartime Frame In Peacetime** [warn] — Consensus-building, broad exploration, and process are correct when the constraint is opportunity and wrong when the constraint is survival — and vice versa. Teams keep whichever mode they last succeeded in, so the mismatch persists exactly through the period where it is most expensive. TRIGGER: An existential condition is stated (runway, a competitor shipping the core feature, a broken primary flow, zero users) and the proposed response is a broad multi-track plan, a process change, or a round of alignment.
