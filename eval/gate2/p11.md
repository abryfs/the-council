# p11

**Prompt:**
> signup to first-action conversion is 22%. people create an account and then just... stop. I don't know if it's the empty state, the fact that we ask for a repo connection immediately, or something dumber. help me figure out what to look at first

_Context: Onboarding funnel is leaking and the founder can see it in the numbers_


## Candidates (8)

- **Exit Reason Assumed** [note] — Churn explanations default to the most legible variable — price, salary, performance — because it is the one already instrumented. The actual reasons are relational and only obtainable by asking, so the retention fix targets the wrong lever and produces no change, which is then read as the problem being intractable. TRIGGER: An explanation of attrition, churn, drop-off, or non-adoption asserts a cause (price, speed, missing feature) without citing anything the departed users or teammates actually said.
- **Cold Start Ignored** [block] — Any product whose value comes from other people's content or presence is worthless to user one, so the acquisition funnel and the retention mechanism fight each other: every user you bring sees an empty room and leaves, and their departure makes the room emptier. The founders must manufacture the initial density by hand because no channel can outrun an empty product. TRIGGER: A spec for a feed, map, marketplace, roster, leaderboard, directory, team view, or any social surface, with no stated plan for what a first user or solo user sees.
- **Skipping The Unscalable** [warn] — Automating the first-user experience before doing it by hand encodes your guesses about what users need into software, and the automation then hides the disconfirming evidence. Manual onboarding is not inefficiency; it is the only instrument fine-grained enough to find the problem that the metrics summarize away. TRIGGER: A plan to build self-serve signup, an onboarding wizard, an email drip, an in-app tour, or a help center before roughly ten users have been onboarded by a founder in person.
- **Premature Automation** [warn] — Automating a process before doing it manually encodes a guess about the rules into code, and the code then prevents you from observing the exceptions that would have taught you the real rules. Manual work at small N is not a stopgap, it is the only instrument that reads the requirement. TRIGGER: A spec automates onboarding, setup, matching, curation, routing, or support before that process has been performed by hand enough times to have written down the exception cases.
- **Segment-Blind PMF Survey** [warn] — The Sean Ellis 40% test averages across everyone who signed up, and a product with intense fit for a narrow segment plus indifference from tourists scores the same as a product nobody loves. The average hides the segment that would tell you what to build, so teams conclude they have no fit and pivot away from the users who actually had it. TRIGGER: A proposal to run the 40% test, NPS, or a satisfaction survey across the full user list, or a stated PMF/NPS number reported as one figure with no segment breakdown.
- **Curated Demo Data** [warn] — Designs validated on hand-picked content encode assumptions about length, density, freshness, and correctness that real data violates on day one; the demo proves the layout, not the product, and the gap surfaces publicly at launch scale. TRIGGER: A design, screenshot, or demo uses seeded, curated, or short sample data; or a spec has no defined empty state, long-string, stale-data, error, and high-volume case for the surface being built.
- **Growth Before Retention** [block] — Acquisition spend against a leaky product buys a number, not a business: each cohort churns at the same rate, so the spend must repeat forever, and headcount and infrastructure scale to the peak while revenue scales to the retained fraction. The growth curve also hides the retention problem from the people funding it. TRIGGER: A plan proposes launch, marketing, headcount, or scale spend while retention, repeat usage, or activation is unmeasured, known-bad, or measured on a single cohort.
- **The Metric That Moves Alone** [warn] — A proxy metric that can rise without the underlying job being served will be optimized directly, because it is the thing that is measured and reported; the product then drifts toward whatever produces the number, and the gap between metric and job widens fastest exactly when the metric looks best. TRIGGER: A success criterion is a count that can increase while the underlying job goes unserved — events emitted, broadcasts sent, tool calls, DAU, sign-ups, page views — with no paired measure of whether the recipient acted on it.
