# p15

**Prompt:**
> the test suite takes 11 minutes and about one in five runs fails on something unrelated to the change. I'm starting to just rerun until green which is obviously bad. fix the flakes first or speed it up first?

_Context: Test suite has become slow and flaky, blocking merges_


## Candidates (5)

- **Guessing The Bottleneck** [warn] — Intuition about performance is trained on the code you read, while cost lives in behavior you cannot see — allocation, cache misses, scheduler and GC intervals, tail latency. Optimizing before profiling spends effort on the wrong axis and hides the real cause behind a changed baseline. TRIGGER: A diff or plan doing performance work — caching, batching, async, index, language/runtime swap — with no profile, benchmark, or production measurement cited, or a claim that something "should be faster".
- **Alert As The Fix** [warn] — Adding a warning to an unactioned stream does not create action; it raises the base rate of ignorable signals, which lowers the probability that any specific one is investigated. The detection exists on paper and is absent in practice. TRIGGER: A proposed remediation that consists of logging a warning, emitting a metric, or adding a monitor — with no owner, threshold, or defined response — or a codebase with existing warnings nobody triages.
- **Root Cause Singular** [warn] — Five-whys terminates at the first cause that is both technical and fixable, because that node is where the search stops feeling productive. The organizational conditions that let the technical fault reach production are upstream of that stopping point, so they survive the fix and reproduce it. TRIGGER: An incident writeup, postmortem, or bug analysis names exactly one root cause and one code-level fix, with no finding about how the defect passed review or shipped.
- **Craft On The Wrong Axis** [warn] — Engineering and design effort has a fixed budget and no automatic mapping to user-perceived value; teams that measure craft by internal difficulty will pour it into components whose quality is invisible in use, and ship a product whose expensive parts are the ones nobody experiences. TRIGGER: A diff or spec invests heavy effort in something the user never sees, touches, or measures — an internal abstraction, a component's hidden tolerances, a custom build pipeline, a rendering engine — while the user-facing behavior is unchanged.
- **Statistics Against Trust** [block] — Answering a reported defect with its probability treats the user's problem as frequency when the actual damage is to the belief that the product's outputs can be relied on without checking. The probability argument is usually correct and always escalates, because it tells the reporter their experience does not count. TRIGGER: A response to a user-reported bug, data-loss report, or wrong-output complaint leads with how rare the condition is, or proposes closing it as won't-fix on frequency grounds.
