# p17

**Prompt:**
> honestly I think we've built too much. There are 9 features in the app and I'd guess 3 get used. I don't know how to decide what to cut without feeling like I'm throwing away work. Walk me through it.

_Context: Six months in, feature list keeps growing and shipping keeps slipping_


## Candidates (7)

- **Refusing The Amputation** [warn] — Deferring a feature to 'phase 2' keeps it in the architecture — its data model, its surface area, its coupling — so the v1 carries the full complexity cost while delivering none of the value. Deletion is the only scope cut that actually reduces work; deferral is bookkeeping. TRIGGER: A v1 spec preserves every feature from a prototype or predecessor, and the scope-cut discussion produces deferrals ('phase 2', 'later slice', 'roadmap') rather than removals from the model.
- **Sunk Identity** [warn] — A decision defended by prior investment or by "this is what we are" cannot be evaluated, because both premises are facts about the past rather than about the option. The incumbent option gets an unearned advantage precisely in proportion to how much was spent establishing it. TRIGGER: A decision or spec is defended by how much has already been built, how long it took, or by identity language ("we're a X company", "this is our thesis"), rather than by the option's forward value.
- **Product Line Sprawl** [warn] — Each additional surface, SKU, or entry point consumes maintenance, documentation, support, and decision bandwidth permanently, while the marginal one is almost always worse than the median — so average quality falls monotonically as count rises, and the team's best people are spread across things no one would start today. TRIGGER: The plan adds a new surface, product, SKU, CLI command, MCP tool, page, or entry point while existing ones are unfinished, unowned, or undocumented; a countable surface inventory grows.
- **Sustaining-Only Roadmap** [warn] — Every roadmap item that improves the product for your best existing customers is individually rational and collectively fatal, because the customers who fund you today also define the axis on which you cannot be disrupted — and the cheaper, worse alternative improves along a different axis until it is good enough. The signal to move never arrives from your own revenue. TRIGGER: Every roadmap item deepens the product for existing or highest-paying users, and a cheaper, simpler, or lower-fidelity alternative in the market is dismissed as a toy or 'not our customer'.
- **Exit Reason Assumed** [note] — Churn explanations default to the most legible variable — price, salary, performance — because it is the one already instrumented. The actual reasons are relational and only obtainable by asking, so the retention fix targets the wrong lever and produces no change, which is then read as the problem being intractable. TRIGGER: An explanation of attrition, churn, drop-off, or non-adoption asserts a cause (price, speed, missing feature) without citing anything the departed users or teammates actually said.
- **Discoverability Mistaken For Absence** [note] — Users request features they cannot find, so a request queue reads as a gap list when it is often a findability report; building the requested thing a second time adds surface area without adding capability and makes the original even harder to find. TRIGGER: A ticket or spec proposes building a capability that may already exist somewhere in the product — "add a way to X," "users can't do Y" — without a check of whether X/Y already ships behind a menu, flag, or command.
- **Numbers Nobody Measured** [block] — A quantitative claim with no measurement behind it still functions as one downstream — it gets cited, budgeted against, and used to close arguments — because the number's format carries authority its provenance does not. Organizations generate these numbers where the desired answer is known and the measurement is expensive. TRIGGER: A spec, README, or marketing line quotes a reliability, latency, uptime, accuracy, or coverage figure without naming the measurement that produced it.
