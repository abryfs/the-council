# p09

**Prompt:**
> A prospect said they'd sign up if we had a Slack integration. Just one prospect. Do I build it? It's maybe 3 days of work and I'd have to maintain it forever.

_Context: Considering adding an integration a prospect asked for_


## Candidates (8, ranked by lexical match)

- **Free Tier With No Wall** [warn] — A free plan converts only where its limit sits exactly at the point a serious user becomes dependent; limits on axes casual users never hit (storage, history, time) give away the whole product and leave the paid tier selling nothing. Fixing this later means taking something back from existing users, which is a far more expensive act than setting the wall correctly at launch. TRIGGER: A spec adding a free plan, free tier, or open beta whose limits are storage, seats-of-one, retention window, or time, rather than the specific capability a committed user cannot work without; also any "free forever" framing.
- **Silent Room Consensus** [warn] — Silence in a decision meeting is recorded as agreement, but it is more often the cost of dissent exceeding its expected payoff — one person's objection cannot beat an apparently settled room, so no one pays. The group then treats the absence of objection as independent corroboration it never received. TRIGGER: A prompt or doc asserts "the team is aligned", "no objections were raised", or "we all agreed" without naming a specific person who argued the other side and what they said.
- **Big-Bang Delivery** [block] — When integration is scheduled once at the end, every incorrect assumption compounds silently for the whole project and all of them surface simultaneously, at the moment there is no budget left to act on them. Nothing is falsified until it is too late to be cheap. TRIGGER: A roadmap or plan whose milestones are all internal (design, build, integrate) with a single user-visible cutover at the end, and no shippable increment before it.
- **Exit Reason Assumed** [note] — Churn explanations default to the most legible variable — price, salary, performance — because it is the one already instrumented. The actual reasons are relational and only obtainable by asking, so the retention fix targets the wrong lever and produces no change, which is then read as the problem being intractable. TRIGGER: An explanation of attrition, churn, drop-off, or non-adoption asserts a cause (price, speed, missing feature) without citing anything the departed users or teammates actually said.
- **Preference-First Framing** [warn] — When the decision-maker states their conclusion before soliciting input, every subsequent contribution is scored by the contributor against the cost of contradicting them, not against the evidence. Input arrives, so the process looks consultative, but it has been filtered to what survives disagreement with the boss. TRIGGER: A request to review, sign off on, or implement an approach whose author has already stated a preferred outcome, or a message shaped as "here's what we're doing — thoughts?"
- **One-Way Door, Casual** [warn] — Decisions get processed at the weight of their immediate cost, not their reversibility, so a cheap-to-decline strategic option receives less scrutiny than an expensive-to-build feature. The irreversibility only becomes visible after the window that would have let you act on it has closed. TRIGGER: A decision that forecloses a future option (a partnership declined, a public API committed, a naming/positioning lock, a data model that cannot be migrated) is being made in the same pass as routine work, with no note distinguishing it.
- **The Metric That Moves Alone** [warn] — A proxy metric that can rise without the underlying job being served will be optimized directly, because it is the thing that is measured and reported; the product then drifts toward whatever produces the number, and the gap between metric and job widens fastest exactly when the metric looks best. TRIGGER: A success criterion is a count that can increase while the underlying job goes unserved — events emitted, broadcasts sent, tool calls, DAU, sign-ups, page views — with no paired measure of whether the recipient acted on it.
- **Distributed Monolith** [warn] — Splitting a codebase into services that share a release cadence and shared libraries converts in-process function calls into network calls without decoupling deploys, so you pay partial-failure, latency, and operational cost while keeping every coupling you had. TRIGGER: A spec proposing a separate service, worker, repo, or deployable per entity/destination/integration, or a diff introducing an RPC boundary between two things that ship together.
