# p05

**Prompt:**
> My cofounder wants to rewrite the backend in Rust. His argument is the Node service is already showing latency problems under load and we'll have to do it eventually anyway. I think it's a 6 week detour. Help me think about this — and if I'm wrong I want to know.

_Context: Cofounder disagreement about the backend stack, mid-week_


## Candidates (8, ranked by lexical match)

- **Stated Preference Trap** [warn] — Users accurately report what they want and cannot report what it costs, because the cost (latency, density, decision load) is a property of the system, not of the wish. Delivering the requested thing literally can move the metric the wrong way. TRIGGER: A spec justifies a feature with "users asked for it," a feature-request count, survey results, or a support-ticket theme — with no statement of what the addition costs in load time, screen density, or steps.
- **Second-System Rewrite** [block] — A rewrite is scoped against the accumulated wishlist of everything the old system got wrong, so the new system is strictly larger than the one that already took years. Meanwhile the old system keeps shipping nothing, and the rewrite's real cost is the undocumented edge cases embedded in the code being discarded. TRIGGER: A proposal to rewrite a working module or surface from scratch, justified by cruft, where the new version is also scoped to fix known limitations of the old one.
- **Redesign Riding A Rewrite** [block] — Shipping a new surface on top of a new backend at once destroys attribution and rollback simultaneously: you cannot tell whether users hate the design or the bugs, and you cannot revert the design because the old one no longer has a system underneath it. TRIGGER: A plan couples a visual/IA redesign with a platform migration, framework swap, schema change, or API rewrite in one release — or the rollback plan for a UI change is "revert the deploy" when the data model also changed.
- **Distributed Monolith** [warn] — Splitting a codebase into services that share a release cadence and shared libraries converts in-process function calls into network calls without decoupling deploys, so you pay partial-failure, latency, and operational cost while keeping every coupling you had. TRIGGER: A spec proposing a separate service, worker, repo, or deployable per entity/destination/integration, or a diff introducing an RPC boundary between two things that ship together.
- **Reused Component, New Envelope** [block] — A component's correctness depends on the operating envelope it was validated in — value ranges, clock rates, load profile. Reuse carries the code but not the envelope, and the assumption that made it safe becomes silently false. TRIGGER: A diff or plan that lifts an existing module, service, query, or library into a new scale, platform, region, or traffic profile described as reusing it "unchanged" or "as-is".
- **Cofounder Equity Without Vesting** [block] — Equity granted without vesting or a cliff prices a contribution before it has happened, and the grant survives the contributor's departure, disagreement, or non-performance. The dispute surfaces at the financing or acquisition that makes the equity worth litigating, which is the worst possible moment to have it. TRIGGER: A cofounder split, advisor grant, contractor equity arrangement, or early-employee offer is described with a percentage but no vesting schedule, cliff, or repurchase right.
- **Launch Without Load** [block] — Capacity assumptions that are never measured are wrong in the unsafe direction, because designers estimate from the intended usage rather than the arrival pattern. Load appears as a step function at launch, not a ramp, so the first real test is also the first outage. TRIGGER: A ship/launch date with no load or soak test in the plan, or a stated concurrency/throughput number that is an estimate rather than a measurement.
- **The Famous Forecaster** [note] — Confidence and reputation are produced by a different process than accuracy, and in public settings they are rewarded more, so the loudest forecast is systematically the least hedged. Without a resolution date and a scoring rule, no forecast ever gets marked wrong, and the calibration signal never reaches anyone. TRIGGER: A claim about the future (adoption, competitor behavior, market timing, "users will want X") is backed by seniority, conviction, or a cited authority rather than by a dated, falsifiable statement someone will check.
