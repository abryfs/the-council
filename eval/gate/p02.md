# p02

**Prompt:**
> Should we build the team/multi-seat plan before launch or ship single-user first? Right now billing assumes one person per account and adding seats means touching auth, the invite flow, and the Stripe integration. I keep going back and forth. We have no paying customers yet.

_Context: Two weeks from a small launch, deciding what makes the cut_


## Candidates (8, ranked by lexical match)

- **Free Tier With No Wall** [warn] — A free plan converts only where its limit sits exactly at the point a serious user becomes dependent; limits on axes casual users never hit (storage, history, time) give away the whole product and leave the paid tier selling nothing. Fixing this later means taking something back from existing users, which is a far more expensive act than setting the wall correctly at launch. TRIGGER: A spec adding a free plan, free tier, or open beta whose limits are storage, seats-of-one, retention window, or time, rather than the specific capability a committed user cannot work without; also any "free forever" framing.
- **Seat Pricing Punishes Adoption** [warn] — When a product's value rises with how many colleagues are on it but the price also rises per colleague, the buyer's rational move is to restrict the rollout — which starves the exact network effect the product depends on. The pricing model actively fights the product's mechanism, and the resulting low penetration reads to the team as weak demand. TRIGGER: A pricing page or spec charging per seat, per user, or per member for a product whose core value requires a whole team, or charging full price for read-only/viewer/observer roles.
- **Big-Bang Delivery** [block] — When integration is scheduled once at the end, every incorrect assumption compounds silently for the whole project and all of them surface simultaneously, at the moment there is no budget left to act on them. Nothing is falsified until it is too late to be cheap. TRIGGER: A roadmap or plan whose milestones are all internal (design, build, integrate) with a single user-visible cutover at the end, and no shippable increment before it.
- **Retroactive Value-Metric Change** [block] — Customers price their own business models on your billing unit; changing that unit — especially applying it to work already shipped — invalidates their planning and reads as a breach rather than a price rise. The revolt costs more in trust and churn than the new revenue was ever worth, because the affected customers are by definition your most successful ones. TRIGGER: A pricing change that alters the billing unit (seats→usage, revenue→installs, flat→metered), reprices existing contracts, or applies to activity that already happened.
- **Skipping The Unscalable** [warn] — Automating the first-user experience before doing it by hand encodes your guesses about what users need into software, and the automation then hides the disconfirming evidence. Manual onboarding is not inefficiency; it is the only instrument fine-grained enough to find the problem that the metrics summarize away. TRIGGER: A plan to build self-serve signup, an onboarding wizard, an email drip, an in-app tour, or a help center before roughly ten users have been onboarded by a founder in person.
- **No Beachhead** [warn] — A message aimed at everyone is specific to no one, so it fails the only test that matters early: can one user explain it to the next user in a sentence. Without a defined first customer, price, channel, and objection-handling all become guesses, and there is no dense population in which word of mouth can propagate. TRIGGER: TAM-driven sizing, "for any team / any company / everyone who...", or a launch plan listing three or more personas with no ranking of which one ships first.
- **Wartime Frame In Peacetime** [warn] — Consensus-building, broad exploration, and process are correct when the constraint is opportunity and wrong when the constraint is survival — and vice versa. Teams keep whichever mode they last succeeded in, so the mismatch persists exactly through the period where it is most expensive. TRIGGER: An existential condition is stated (runway, a competitor shipping the core feature, a broken primary flow, zero users) and the proposed response is a broad multi-track plan, a process change, or a round of alignment.
- **Friction Placed For Revenue** [block] — Placing confirm/cancel affordances or defaults to favor the business converts an interface into a trap: the same visual language that means "proceed" elsewhere now means "you were charged," and the resulting revenue is legally recoverable and reputationally permanent. TRIGGER: A diff changes default selections, button placement/prominence, cancel-vs-confirm ordering, or opt-out wording in a purchase, subscription, consent, sharing, or account-deletion flow in a way that favors the business.
