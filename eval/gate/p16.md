# p16

**Prompt:**
> timestamps are showing up one day off for users in Australia. we store UTC and format client side with toLocaleDateString. find it

_Context: Small formatting bug reported by a user in a different timezone_


## Candidates (8, ranked by lexical match)

- **Built On Rented Land** [warn] — A distribution channel owned by another company is revocable on their schedule, and they revoke it exactly when you become large enough to matter or when they want the market themselves. The dependency is invisible during the growth phase because the channel is working, which is the same evidence that makes you a threat. TRIGGER: A growth mechanism that depends on another company's API, social graph, app-store placement, feed distribution, marketplace listing, extension store, or OAuth integration as the primary source of new users.
- **Interface Without A Contract** [warn] — When two components exchange bare numbers or strings, the meaning (unit, timezone, encoding, ID namespace) lives only in each side's head. Nothing at the boundary can detect disagreement, so the mismatch propagates as plausible-looking data instead of an error. TRIGGER: A new API field, event payload, or cross-team/cross-service boundary passing raw numbers, timestamps, identifiers, or strings with no shared type, unit, or validation at the boundary.
- **Funding As Distribution** [warn] — A funding announcement and its press cycle produce a one-time spike of curious non-customers, which the team reads as demand and uses to justify skipping channel-building. Press has no compounding mechanism — it cannot be re-run — so the traffic decays to zero while the burn rate raised to match the ambition does not. TRIGGER: A launch plan whose top-of-funnel is press coverage, a funding announcement, an influencer/analyst push, or a Product Hunt / HN / Show HN day, with no channel that keeps producing users on day 30.
- **Silent Room Consensus** [warn] — Silence in a decision meeting is recorded as agreement, but it is more often the cost of dissent exceeding its expected payoff — one person's objection cannot beat an apparently settled room, so no one pays. The group then treats the absence of objection as independent corroboration it never received. TRIGGER: A prompt or doc asserts "the team is aligned", "no objections were raised", or "we all agreed" without naming a specific person who argued the other side and what they said.
- **One-Way Door Misread** [block] — Anything external parties can build against — a public API, tool name, URL scheme, event type, wire field, file format — becomes unrevocable the moment someone depends on it, because the cost of removal is paid by them and shows up as broken trust rather than a rollback. Such decisions get made at the speed of a reversible one because the diff looks small. TRIGGER: A diff or spec publishes something external parties will depend on: a public API endpoint, an MCP or CLI tool name, an event type or enum value, a URL scheme, a config key, an on-disk or wire format.
- **Discoverability Mistaken For Absence** [note] — Users request features they cannot find, so a request queue reads as a gap list when it is often a findability report; building the requested thing a second time adds surface area without adding capability and makes the original even harder to find. TRIGGER: A ticket or spec proposes building a capability that may already exist somewhere in the product — "add a way to X," "users can't do Y" — without a check of whether X/Y already ships behind a menu, flag, or command.
- **The Big-Bang Relaunch** [block] — Replacing a shipped surface wholesale in one release couples a hundred independent bets into a single irreversible event, so you cannot tell which change caused the loss, and existing users pay the full re-learning cost on a day they did not choose. Habitual users are the ones who notice most, and they are the ones you can least afford to lose. TRIGGER: A plan replaces an existing shipped surface in one release — 'v2 replaces v1', 'retire the old frame', 'cut over' — with no dual-run, opt-in, or staged migration.
- **Relearning Tax Ignored** [warn] — In a product people use daily, the interface is a motor habit; moving things resets that habit for every existing user at once, so the cost is paid on day one by everyone while the benefit accrues slowly to newcomers who do not exist yet. The new-user gain almost never exceeds the aggregate relearning loss. TRIGGER: A spec reorganizes navigation, moves an existing feature to a new location, renames a primary action, or changes the information architecture of a product that already has habituated daily users.
