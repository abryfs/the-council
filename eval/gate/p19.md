# p19

**Prompt:**
> we've been free for 3 months and I've been too scared to put a price on it. 30ish active users, a handful use it daily. Should I start charging now, and if so how do I not lose the people who are actually using it?

_Context: Deciding whether to charge, weeks after a free beta started_


## Candidates (8, ranked by lexical match)

- **Relearning Tax Ignored** [warn] — In a product people use daily, the interface is a motor habit; moving things resets that habit for every existing user at once, so the cost is paid on day one by everyone while the benefit accrues slowly to newcomers who do not exist yet. The new-user gain almost never exceeds the aggregate relearning loss. TRIGGER: A spec reorganizes navigation, moves an existing feature to a new location, renames a primary action, or changes the information architecture of a product that already has habituated daily users.
- **Seat Pricing Punishes Adoption** [warn] — When a product's value rises with how many colleagues are on it but the price also rises per colleague, the buyer's rational move is to restrict the rollout — which starves the exact network effect the product depends on. The pricing model actively fights the product's mechanism, and the resulting low penetration reads to the team as weak demand. TRIGGER: A pricing page or spec charging per seat, per user, or per member for a product whose core value requires a whole team, or charging full price for read-only/viewer/observer roles.
- **Exit Reason Assumed** [note] — Churn explanations default to the most legible variable — price, salary, performance — because it is the one already instrumented. The actual reasons are relational and only obtainable by asking, so the retention fix targets the wrong lever and produces no change, which is then read as the problem being intractable. TRIGGER: An explanation of attrition, churn, drop-off, or non-adoption asserts a cause (price, speed, missing feature) without citing anything the departed users or teammates actually said.
- **Undifferentiated Price War** [warn] — Competing on a dimension your rival can copy within a week converts the whole category's margin into customer subsidy, and both sides must keep spending to hold position they can never win. The winner is whoever has more capital, not whoever built the better product. TRIGGER: The plan competes on price, free-tier size, limits, discounting, or speed of copying a feature, in a market where a funded competitor can match the move immediately.
- **Channel-Price Mismatch** [block] — A product's price point determines which acquisition channels can ever pay back; ad-based channels have a floor CAC set by auction competition, and if that floor exceeds the customer's lifetime contribution the channel is unusable at any spend level. Teams discover this after burning the test budget because they benchmarked click-through, not payback. TRIGGER: A GTM plan or budget line proposing Google/Meta/LinkedIn/Reddit ads, sponsorships, or outbound SDRs without stating ARPU, gross margin, and a payback period in months.
- **Job Mistaken For Demographic** [note] — Segmenting by who the user is produces feature requests that correlate with the segment but explain nothing causal, so improvements aimed at the persona miss the situation that actually triggered the purchase. Two people in the same demographic hire the product for opposite jobs, and averaging them produces a product for neither. TRIGGER: Requirements are segmented by who the user is (role, persona, seniority, company size, 'founders vs engineers') rather than by the situation and task at the moment of use.
- **Free Tier With No Wall** [warn] — A free plan converts only where its limit sits exactly at the point a serious user becomes dependent; limits on axes casual users never hit (storage, history, time) give away the whole product and leave the paid tier selling nothing. Fixing this later means taking something back from existing users, which is a far more expensive act than setting the wall correctly at launch. TRIGGER: A spec adding a free plan, free tier, or open beta whose limits are storage, seats-of-one, retention window, or time, rather than the specific capability a committed user cannot work without; also any "free forever" framing.
- **Cold Start Ignored** [block] — Any product whose value comes from other people's content or presence is worthless to user one, so the acquisition funnel and the retention mechanism fight each other: every user you bring sees an empty room and leaves, and their departure makes the room emptier. The founders must manufacture the initial density by hand because no channel can outrun an empty product. TRIGGER: A spec for a feed, map, marketplace, roster, leaderboard, directory, team view, or any social surface, with no stated plan for what a first user or solo user sees.
