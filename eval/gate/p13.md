# p13

**Prompt:**
> read the copy on the landing page and tell me honestly if a stranger would understand what this does in ten seconds. I've read it so many times I can't tell anymore. don't be nice about it

_Context: Reviewing a landing page draft the night before sharing it_


## Candidates (8, ranked by lexical match)

- **Seat Pricing Punishes Adoption** [warn] — When a product's value rises with how many colleagues are on it but the price also rises per colleague, the buyer's rational move is to restrict the rollout — which starves the exact network effect the product depends on. The pricing model actively fights the product's mechanism, and the resulting low penetration reads to the team as weak demand. TRIGGER: A pricing page or spec charging per seat, per user, or per member for a product whose core value requires a whole team, or charging full price for read-only/viewer/observer roles.
- **Feature Parity Positioning** [warn] — Positioning your product as a better version of an incumbent asks the customer to compare on the incumbent's chosen axes, where the incumbent's scale wins by construction, and it gives no one a reason to bear switching cost. Parity is also unbounded work: matching the feature list is a treadmill the incumbent sets the speed of. TRIGGER: A positioning doc, landing page, or spec that frames the product as "X but better/faster/cheaper", or leads with a feature-comparison table against a named incumbent.
- **Premature Automation** [warn] — Automating a process before doing it manually encodes a guess about the rules into code, and the code then prevents you from observing the exceptions that would have taught you the real rules. Manual work at small N is not a stopgap, it is the only instrument that reads the requirement. TRIGGER: A spec automates onboarding, setup, matching, curation, routing, or support before that process has been performed by hand enough times to have written down the exception cases.
- **Skipping The Unscalable** [warn] — Automating the first-user experience before doing it by hand encodes your guesses about what users need into software, and the automation then hides the disconfirming evidence. Manual onboarding is not inefficiency; it is the only instrument fine-grained enough to find the problem that the metrics summarize away. TRIGGER: A plan to build self-serve signup, an onboarding wizard, an email drip, an in-app tour, or a help center before roughly ten users have been onboarded by a founder in person.
- **The Untested Restore** [block] — Backups are write-path artifacts; nothing exercises the read path until an emergency, so silent failures (empty buckets, wrong flags, expired credentials) accumulate undetected. The existence of a backup job is not evidence that a restore is possible. TRIGGER: A spec, runbook, or reply that mentions backups, snapshots, replicas, or disaster recovery as mitigation, with no restore drill or verification step.
- **The Big-Bang Relaunch** [block] — Replacing a shipped surface wholesale in one release couples a hundred independent bets into a single irreversible event, so you cannot tell which change caused the loss, and existing users pay the full re-learning cost on a day they did not choose. Habitual users are the ones who notice most, and they are the ones you can least afford to lose. TRIGGER: A plan replaces an existing shipped surface in one release — 'v2 replaces v1', 'retire the old frame', 'cut over' — with no dual-run, opt-in, or staged migration.
- **Segment-Blind PMF Survey** [warn] — The Sean Ellis 40% test averages across everyone who signed up, and a product with intense fit for a narrow segment plus indifference from tourists scores the same as a product nobody loves. The average hides the segment that would tell you what to build, so teams conclude they have no fit and pivot away from the users who actually had it. TRIGGER: A proposal to run the 40% test, NPS, or a satisfaction survey across the full user list, or a stated PMF/NPS number reported as one figure with no segment breakdown.
- **Redesign Riding A Rewrite** [block] — Shipping a new surface on top of a new backend at once destroys attribution and rollback simultaneously: you cannot tell whether users hate the design or the bugs, and you cannot revert the design because the old one no longer has a system underneath it. TRIGGER: A plan couples a visual/IA redesign with a platform migration, framework swap, schema change, or API rewrite in one release — or the rollback plan for a UI change is "revert the deploy" when the data model also changed.
