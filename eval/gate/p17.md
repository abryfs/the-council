# p17

**Prompt:**
> honestly I think we've built too much. There are 9 features in the app and I'd guess 3 get used. I don't know how to decide what to cut without feeling like I'm throwing away work. Walk me through it.

_Context: Six months in, feature list keeps growing and shipping keeps slipping_


## Candidates (8, ranked by lexical match)

- **Sunk Identity** [warn] — A decision defended by prior investment or by "this is what we are" cannot be evaluated, because both premises are facts about the past rather than about the option. The incumbent option gets an unearned advantage precisely in proportion to how much was spent establishing it. TRIGGER: A decision or spec is defended by how much has already been built, how long it took, or by identity language ("we're a X company", "this is our thesis"), rather than by the option's forward value.
- **Second-System Effect** [block] — The v2 becomes the dumping ground for every idea deferred from v1, and because none of them has shipped, none can be cut on evidence. Scope grows faster than the team can integrate, so the system never reaches a state where any single feature is finishable. TRIGGER: A spec or plan for a v2/next-gen version that lists features deferred from v1, or bundles a new data model + new UI + new extensibility layer in one release.
- **Skipping The Unscalable** [warn] — Automating the first-user experience before doing it by hand encodes your guesses about what users need into software, and the automation then hides the disconfirming evidence. Manual onboarding is not inefficiency; it is the only instrument fine-grained enough to find the problem that the metrics summarize away. TRIGGER: A plan to build self-serve signup, an onboarding wizard, an email drip, an in-app tour, or a help center before roughly ten users have been onboarded by a founder in person.
- **Preserving The Shape** [warn] — When a change is bolted onto an existing structure specifically to avoid disturbing it — because touching it would trigger a migration, recertification, or retraining cost — the new behavior inherits constraints it was never designed for, and the reason to avoid them becomes a reason not to fix them. TRIGGER: A change explicitly scoped to avoid touching an existing schema, interface, migration, or retraining/re-certification cost — phrases like "without changing the existing", "to avoid a migration", "keep it backward compatible" driving the design rather than following it.
- **Built On Rented Land** [warn] — A distribution channel owned by another company is revocable on their schedule, and they revoke it exactly when you become large enough to matter or when they want the market themselves. The dependency is invisible during the growth phase because the channel is working, which is the same evidence that makes you a threat. TRIGGER: A growth mechanism that depends on another company's API, social graph, app-store placement, feed distribution, marketplace listing, extension store, or OAuth integration as the primary source of new users.
- **Refusing The Amputation** [warn] — Deferring a feature to 'phase 2' keeps it in the architecture — its data model, its surface area, its coupling — so the v1 carries the full complexity cost while delivering none of the value. Deletion is the only scope cut that actually reduces work; deferral is bookkeeping. TRIGGER: A v1 spec preserves every feature from a prototype or predecessor, and the scope-cut discussion produces deferrals ('phase 2', 'later slice', 'roadmap') rather than removals from the model.
- **Free Tier With No Wall** [warn] — A free plan converts only where its limit sits exactly at the point a serious user becomes dependent; limits on axes casual users never hit (storage, history, time) give away the whole product and leave the paid tier selling nothing. Fixing this later means taking something back from existing users, which is a far more expensive act than setting the wall correctly at launch. TRIGGER: A spec adding a free plan, free tier, or open beta whose limits are storage, seats-of-one, retention window, or time, rather than the specific capability a committed user cannot work without; also any "free forever" framing.
- **Adding People To A Late Thing** [warn] — New people on a late project consume the time of the people who know the system, and communication paths grow quadratically with headcount, so the near-term effect on a component with tight internal coupling is negative. Partitionable work is the exception, and the work that is late is rarely the partitionable kind. TRIGGER: A slipping deliverable is answered by assigning more engineers, more parallel agents, or more concurrent sessions to the same component or file set, rather than by cutting scope.
