# p04

**Prompt:**
> we have 4 users. two of them are me and my cofounder. what now

_Context: Post-launch reality check after a Show HN that went nowhere_


## Candidates (8, ranked by lexical match)

- **Cofounder Equity Without Vesting** [block] — Equity granted without vesting or a cliff prices a contribution before it has happened, and the grant survives the contributor's departure, disagreement, or non-performance. The dispute surfaces at the financing or acquisition that makes the equity worth litigating, which is the worst possible moment to have it. TRIGGER: A cofounder split, advisor grant, contractor equity arrangement, or early-employee offer is described with a percentage but no vesting schedule, cliff, or repurchase right.
- **Org Chart Shipped To Users** [warn] — A split that mirrors an internal boundary — separate app, brand, login, repo, or team — makes users pay the cost of a structure that exists for the builders' convenience. Users experience one job; splitting it forces them to re-onboard into a second product to accomplish what was previously one motion. TRIGGER: A split, rename, or separation in the product mirrors an internal boundary (two teams, two repos, two services, two panes) and forces the user to move between surfaces to complete one task.
- **Innovation Token Overdraft** [warn] — Every unfamiliar technology carries unknown failure modes that only surface under production load, and each one consumes the same scarce resource: engineer attention during incidents. Spending several at once means every outage has multiple novel suspects and no one has intuition about any of them. TRIGGER: A diff or spec that introduces a new datastore, language, queue, or orchestration layer — especially two or more in the same change — where an already-running component would do.
- **Distributed Monolith** [warn] — Splitting a codebase into services that share a release cadence and shared libraries converts in-process function calls into network calls without decoupling deploys, so you pay partial-failure, latency, and operational cost while keeping every coupling you had. TRIGGER: A spec proposing a separate service, worker, repo, or deployable per entity/destination/integration, or a diff introducing an RPC boundary between two things that ship together.
- **Hype Ahead Of Usability** [warn] — A long, expert-narrated demo can make an unexplainable product look compelling to an audience primed to be impressed, and invite scarcity converts that impression into signups. Neither tests the actual constraint — whether one ordinary user can explain the product to the next one — so the waitlist converts into a cohort that opens it once and never returns. TRIGGER: A launch plan gated by waitlist or invite codes, or a demo script/video longer than about two minutes that requires narration to make the product legible.
- **Metaphor Fusion** [warn] — A product that fuses several familiar categories inherits none of their mental models, so a new user has no prior to fall back on and no one-sentence description to pass along; adoption then depends on formal explanation, which does not travel. TRIGGER: A spec describes the surface by joining categories with "and" ("it's a doc and a chat and a board"), unifies two existing product surfaces into one, or cannot state what the thing is in one sentence a non-user would repeat.
- **Two-Way Door Deliberated** [note] — Applying heavyweight process to a reversible decision costs more than being wrong would have, and the delay itself removes the cheapest source of information — shipping it and watching. Organizations default to the heavy process for everything because the process was designed around the rare irreversible case. TRIGGER: An easily reversible change — copy, layout, a default value, ordering, a flag's initial setting, a threshold — is escalated to a spec, a review cycle, or a founder decision.
- **One Shell, Two Inputs** [warn] — Touch and pointer imply different target sizes, hover availability, gesture vocabularies, and discovery mechanics; a single interface tuned for both is tuned for neither, and hidden gestures that are natural on a tablet become undiscoverable on hardware with no edges to swipe. TRIGGER: A spec targets one UI across incompatible input modes or form factors — touch and mouse, mobile and desktop, TV and handheld — or introduces hover-dependent or edge-gesture affordances in a surface that must also work with the other input.
