# p03

**Prompt:**
> the settings page feels cluttered and I hate looking at it. there are like 14 rows now and half of them are things nobody touches. clean it up

_Context: Just came back to a settings screen that has grown organically for months_


## Candidates (8, ranked by lexical match)

- **Unlabeled Working Surface** [warn] — Surfaces "no user sees" — shell prompts, admin panels, log output, internal dashboards — are used by humans under time pressure, and identical-looking contexts there produce identity confusion at the exact moment stakes are highest. Unseen does not mean unused; it means untested. TRIGGER: Work touches an internal-only surface — CLI output, shell/SSH tooling, admin console, staging vs prod environment, log lines, migration scripts — and the change is justified as "internal, no design needed."
- **Craft On The Wrong Axis** [warn] — Engineering and design effort has a fixed budget and no automatic mapping to user-perceived value; teams that measure craft by internal difficulty will pour it into components whose quality is invisible in use, and ship a product whose expensive parts are the ones nobody experiences. TRIGGER: A diff or spec invests heavy effort in something the user never sees, touches, or measures — an internal abstraction, a component's hidden tolerances, a custom build pipeline, a rendering engine — while the user-facing behavior is unchanged.
- **Product Line Sprawl** [warn] — Each additional surface, SKU, or entry point consumes maintenance, documentation, support, and decision bandwidth permanently, while the marginal one is almost always worse than the median — so average quality falls monotonically as count rises, and the team's best people are spread across things no one would start today. TRIGGER: The plan adds a new surface, product, SKU, CLI command, MCP tool, page, or entry point while existing ones are unfinished, unowned, or undocumented; a countable surface inventory grows.
- **The Great Rewrite** [block] — A rewrite discards the accumulated bug fixes encoded in the old code — each ugly branch is a defect someone found in production — while the old system keeps needing maintenance, so the team pays for two systems and ships neither. TRIGGER: A prompt or issue proposing to "rewrite from scratch", "start clean", "greenfield it", or replace a working subsystem wholesale because the code is messy.
- **Preserving The Shape** [warn] — When a change is bolted onto an existing structure specifically to avoid disturbing it — because touching it would trigger a migration, recertification, or retraining cost — the new behavior inherits constraints it was never designed for, and the reason to avoid them becomes a reason not to fix them. TRIGGER: A change explicitly scoped to avoid touching an existing schema, interface, migration, or retraining/re-certification cost — phrases like "without changing the existing", "to avoid a migration", "keep it backward compatible" driving the design rather than following it.
- **Distributed Monolith** [warn] — Splitting a codebase into services that share a release cadence and shared libraries converts in-process function calls into network calls without decoupling deploys, so you pay partial-failure, latency, and operational cost while keeping every coupling you had. TRIGGER: A spec proposing a separate service, worker, repo, or deployable per entity/destination/integration, or a diff introducing an RPC boundary between two things that ship together.
- **Motion Without An Exit** [warn] — Vestibular responses to large-field motion are physiological, not preferential — a transition that reads as delightful to most users induces genuine nausea and vertigo in a minority, and without a system-level opt-out those users cannot use the product at all. TRIGGER: A diff adds parallax, zoom transitions, autoplay, scroll-linked animation, or page transitions — and does not gate them behind prefers-reduced-motion or an equivalent user setting.
- **Feature Parity Positioning** [warn] — Positioning your product as a better version of an incumbent asks the customer to compare on the incumbent's chosen axes, where the incumbent's scale wins by construction, and it gives no one a reason to bear switching cost. Parity is also unbounded work: matching the feature list is a treadmill the incumbent sets the speed of. TRIGGER: A positioning doc, landing page, or spec that frames the product as "X but better/faster/cheaper", or leads with a feature-comparison table against a named incumbent.
