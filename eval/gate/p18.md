# p18

**Prompt:**
> add retry with exponential backoff and jitter around the webhook dispatcher. cap at 5 attempts, dead-letter after that. write tests

_Context: Adding a small piece of infrastructure while implementing sync_


## Candidates (8, ranked by lexical match)

- **Chesterton's Dead Code** [block] — Code that appears unused is often reachable through a path nobody has in mind — an old flag, a stale server, a legacy client — so removing the guard while leaving the code, or reusing its switch for a new meaning, arms a landmine that only fires under partial deployment. TRIGGER: A diff that deletes or repurposes a feature flag, retry, guard clause, or branch labeled unused/deprecated/legacy without explaining what originally set it.
- **Discoverability Mistaken For Absence** [note] — Users request features they cannot find, so a request queue reads as a gap list when it is often a findability report; building the requested thing a second time adds surface area without adding capability and makes the original even harder to find. TRIGGER: A ticket or spec proposes building a capability that may already exist somewhere in the product — "add a way to X," "users can't do Y" — without a check of whether X/Y already ships behind a menu, flag, or command.
- **Distribution As An Afterthought** [block] — Product work has a legible feedback loop (ship, see it work) and distribution does not, so engineering-led teams spend the entire runway on the loop that feels productive and reach the market with no channel built. By the time distribution becomes urgent, the money that would have funded channel experiments is already spent on the product. TRIGGER: A roadmap, spec, or milestone list where every item is a product capability and no item names a specific acquisition channel, or a plan that says marketing/launch/GTM happens "after v1".
- **The Untested Restore** [block] — Backups are write-path artifacts; nothing exercises the read path until an emergency, so silent failures (empty buckets, wrong flags, expired credentials) accumulate undetected. The existence of a backup job is not evidence that a restore is possible. TRIGGER: A spec, runbook, or reply that mentions backups, snapshots, replicas, or disaster recovery as mitigation, with no restore drill or verification step.
- **Hype Ahead Of Usability** [warn] — A long, expert-narrated demo can make an unexplainable product look compelling to an audience primed to be impressed, and invite scarcity converts that impression into signups. Neither tests the actual constraint — whether one ordinary user can explain the product to the next one — so the waitlist converts into a cohort that opens it once and never returns. TRIGGER: A launch plan gated by waitlist or invite codes, or a demo script/video longer than about two minutes that requires narration to make the product legible.
- **Automatic Failover Split-Brain** [block] — Automated leader promotion cannot distinguish a dead primary from an unreachable one, so a brief network partition produces two writable primaries. The outage is then not the downtime but the reconciliation of divergent writes, which is manual and unbounded. TRIGGER: A design adding automatic failover, leader election, multi-region writes, or a health-check-driven promotion — or any mention of a heartbeat timeout that decides who is authoritative.
- **Two-Way Door Deliberated** [note] — Applying heavyweight process to a reversible decision costs more than being wrong would have, and the delay itself removes the cheapest source of information — shipping it and watching. Organizations default to the heavy process for everything because the process was designed around the rare irreversible case. TRIGGER: An easily reversible change — copy, layout, a default value, ordering, a flag's initial setting, a threshold — is escalated to a spec, a review cycle, or a founder decision.
- **Global Blast Radius** [block] — Treating rules, content, or config as "not code" routes it around the staged rollout that protects code, so a bad artifact reaches 100% of the fleet in one push. If the failure mode also prevents the machine from booting, there is no remote path to roll back. TRIGGER: A change shipping to all users, machines, or tenants simultaneously — or a data/config/rules/prompt/template update path that bypasses the canary and staged-rollout used for code.
